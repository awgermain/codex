Why a melting crab?

The Crabby Melter

The sky

Cancer is one of the twelve constellations of the zodiac and the faintest of them all. Latin for “crab,” it is a shape you have to work hard to find in the northern sky. γ and δ are Asellus Borealis and Asellus Australis, the northern and southern donkeys. Between them sits Messier 44 — Praesepe, the manger they’re feeding from, better known now as the Beehive Cluster. Acubens (α Cancri) marks the crab’s southern claw. The name derives from the Arabic al-zubānā, “the claw.” It’s a multiple star system sitting close enough to the ecliptic that the Moon can pass in front of it and blot it out entirely.

The drink

My grandparents made Manhattans in big red glass tumblers the size of small vases — enormous, unfussy, cheap whiskey and even cheaper vermouth. My father kept the idea though dropped the volume to that of a double rocks glass, upgraded the ingredients for the better, but stopped short of anything you might call refined technique. Two parts Rye or Bourbon, one part Carpano Antica, a little maraschino syrup, at least two cherries, built in the glass over ice and barely stirred … possibly with a finger. He started referring to them as Crabby Melters … Double pours, quick to build, strong, and straight to the point. He will make one to “melt the crab away” … to take the edge off and loosen up.

The connection

This one’s an homage to my father’s unfussy Manhattan. His waggish name for it and the crab connection made a natural pairing, so this app’s mascot pretty much picked itself. As a Cancer who is both rather partial to a Manhattan and can, at times, live up to the crabby nature of my astrological sign, I too like something to help melt that crabby edge away. I guess it runs in the family.
